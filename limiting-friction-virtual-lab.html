Theory: Limiting Friction & Normal Reaction

Limiting Friction ($F$): When a body is at the verge of sliding over a horizontal surface, the maximum opposing force that comes into play is called the force of limiting friction.

$$F \propto R \implies F = \mu_s R \iff \mu_s = \frac{F}{R}$$

where:

  • $F$ = force of limiting friction (total weight in pan at which block just begins to slide)
  • $R$ = normal reaction of the surface on the block ($R = (M + m) g$)
  • $\mu_s$ = coefficient of limiting static friction between the surfaces

Laws of Limiting Friction:

  • The magnitude of limiting friction is directly proportional to the normal reaction ($F \propto R$).
  • The direction of limiting friction is always tangential to the contacting surfaces and opposite to the direction of impending motion.
  • Limiting friction is independent of the apparent area of contact, provided normal reaction remains constant.
  • Limiting friction depends on the nature and roughness of the contacting materials.

$F\text{–}R$ Graph: A plot of limiting friction $F$ on the y-axis against normal reaction $R$ on the x-axis yields a straight line passing through the origin. The slope of this straight line gives the coefficient of friction $\mu_s$:

$$\text{Slope} = \frac{\Delta F}{\Delta R} = \mu_s$$
