Theory: Spherometer & Radius of Curvature

Principle: A spherometer operates on the principle of a precision micrometer screw. The three outer fixed legs form an equilateral triangle with mean side distance $a$. The central screw measures the sagittal height (or depth) $h$ of the spherical cap above or below the plane formed by the outer three tips.

$$\text{Least Count (LC)} = \frac{\text{Pitch}}{\text{Total Circular Scale Divisions (CSD)}}$$

Geometric Derivation: If $r$ is the radius of the circle passing through the three outer legs, by geometry of an equilateral triangle of side $a$:

$$r = \frac{a}{\sqrt{3}}$$

By the intersecting chords theorem of a circle of radius $R$ in cross-section:

$$h (2R - h) = r^2 = \frac{a^2}{3} \implies 2Rh - h^2 = \frac{a^2}{3}$$

Solving for $R$:

$$R = \frac{a^2}{6h} + \frac{h}{2}$$

where $a$ is the mean distance between any two outer legs and $h$ is the sagitta height measured between the spherical surface and the reference plane glass plate.
