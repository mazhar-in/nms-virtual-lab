Ohm's Law: At constant temperature, the electric current $I$ passing through a conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference $V$ across its ends:
$V = I \cdot R \implies \frac{V}{I} = \text{Constant}$

Theory: Verification of Ohm's Law

Statement: The current ($I$) flowing through a metallic conductor is directly proportional to the potential difference ($V$) applied across its ends, provided temperature and mechanical conditions remain unaltered.

Mathematical Formulation:

$$V \propto I \iff V = I R$$

where $R$ is the electrical resistance of the conductor.

Microscopic Relation: The drift velocity of electrons $v_d = \frac{e E \tau}{m}$. The electric current is given by:

$$I = n e A v_d = \left(\frac{n e^2 A \tau}{m L}\right) V \implies R = \frac{m L}{n e^2 A \tau}$$
