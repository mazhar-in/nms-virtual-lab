Theory: Physical Beam Balance

Principle of Moments: A physical beam balance works on the principle of moments. In equilibrium, the clockwise moment produced by weights in the right pan is balanced by the anti-clockwise moment produced by the unknown mass in the left pan.

$$m_1 g \times l_1 = m_2 g \times l_2$$

Where:

  • $m_1$ = mass of the object in the left pan
  • $m_2$ = mass of standard weights in the right pan
  • $l_1, l_2$ = lengths of the left and right arms from the central fulcrum

True Balance: In a perfectly constructed true balance, the arms are of equal length ($l_1 = l_2$). Therefore, when the pointer rests at the zero mark (or oscillates equally on both sides), the moments cancel out, giving:

$$m_1 = m_2$$

Safety Precaution: The beam must always be arrested (lowered to rest off the agate knife-edges) when adding or removing weights or objects to prevent damaging the delicate fulcrum edge.
