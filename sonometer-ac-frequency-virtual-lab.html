📘 Laboratory Manual: Frequency of AC Mains

1. Aim of the Experiment

To determine the frequency of alternating current (AC) mains using a sonometer (by observing the resonant vibration of a stretched wire driven by electromagnetic force).

2. Apparatus Required

  • Sonometer: Hollow resonant wooden sound box with two sound holes, tuning pegs, and a 100 cm graduated scale.
  • Knife-edge Bridges: Two movable wedge-shaped wooden/brass bridges ($A$ and $B$) with sharp apexes to define vibrating wire length $l$.
  • Step-Down Transformer: Steps down 220V mains to safe 2V–6V AC.
  • Magnet / Electromagnet: Strong horseshoe permanent magnet (Method A) or AC electromagnet with soft iron core (Method B).
  • Sonometer Wire: Brass or copper wire (non-magnetic, for Method A) or soft iron wire (ferromagnetic, for Method B).
  • Slotted Weights with Hanger: 500g hanger + seven 500g slotted weights ($0.5\text{ kg to }4.0\text{ kg}$).
  • Light Paper Rider: Inverted V-shaped strip of paper placed at the center (antinode) of the vibrating segment.
  • Screw Gauge & Physical Balance: To measure the wire diameter and determine linear density $m$.

3. Step-by-Step Procedure

  1. Tension Setup: Place a known mass $M$ (e.g., 1.0 kg) on the weight hanger suspended over the frictionless pulley. The tension is $T = Mg$.
  2. Position Magnet: Position the horseshoe magnet (or electromagnet) at the exact midpoint between Bridge A and Bridge B.
  3. Paper Rider Placement: Fold a light paper strip into an inverted V-shape and balance it carefully on the wire at the antinode (midpoint between bridges).
  4. Close AC Switch: Turn on the AC power supply. Alternating current flows, producing a dynamic transverse magnetic driving force on the wire.
  5. Resonance Search (Flutter & Fly-off): Slowly adjust movable Bridge B:
    • When far from resonance, the wire hums quietly and the paper rider sits calmly.
    • As the vibrating length approaches resonant length $l_0$, the wire vibration amplitude grows and the paper rider begins to flutter vigorously.
    • At exact resonance, the amplitude peaks sharply and the paper rider is violently thrown off ("Flies Off!").
  6. Record Resonant Lengths: Record length $l_1$ when approaching resonance by increasing length, and $l_2$ by decreasing length. The mean resonant length is $l = \frac{l_1 + l_2}{2}$.
  7. Repeat for Various Loads: Repeat the observations for loads $M = 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 2.5, 3.0\text{ kg}$.
  8. Tabulate & Plot: Record all readings in the observation table and plot $\sqrt{T}$ against $l$ to determine the frequency from the slope.

4. Precautions

  • Ensure the sonometer wire is kink-free, uniform, and rests snugly on the sharp edges of the bridges.
  • Keep the pulley clean and lubricated to eliminate friction errors.
  • Use low voltage AC (2V–6V) from a step-down transformer to prevent excessive Joule heating of the wire ($I^2Rt$).
  • Place the paper rider precisely at the center between the two bridges where the fundamental antinode forms.

📐 Physics Theory & Mathematical Derivations

1. Transverse Stationary Waves on a Stretched String

When a stretched wire of length $l$, tension $T$, and mass per unit length (linear mass density) $m$ is fixed at both ends by two sharp bridges, transverse standing waves are established with nodes at the bridges and an antinode at the center.

The velocity of transverse waves along the wire is given by:

$$v = \sqrt{\frac{T}{m}}$$

For the fundamental mode of vibration, the length of the vibrating segment corresponds to half a wavelength ($\lambda / 2 = l \implies \lambda = 2l$). Hence, the natural fundamental frequency of the wire is:

$$\nu_0 = \frac{v}{\lambda} = \frac{1}{2l}\sqrt{\frac{T}{m}}$$

2. Dual Methods of Excitation & AC Frequency Relation

Method A: AC Current Through Wire in a Transverse Magnetic Field (Horseshoe Magnet)

When an alternating current $I = I_0 \sin(2\pi f t)$ of mains frequency $f$ passes through a non-magnetic wire (brass or copper) situated in a transverse magnetic field $B$ from a permanent horseshoe magnet, the magnetic Lorentz force acting on the wire is:

$$F(t) = B \cdot I(t) \cdot l = B I_0 l \sin(2\pi f t)$$

By Fleming's Left-Hand Rule, the force acts upwards during one half cycle and downwards during the next half cycle. Therefore, the frequency of the mechanical driving force is identical to the AC mains frequency $f$.

At resonance ($\nu_0 = f$):

$$f = \frac{1}{2l}\sqrt{\frac{T}{m}} \implies \frac{\sqrt{T}}{l} = 2f\sqrt{m} = \text{constant}$$
Method B: Ferromagnetic Wire Under an AC Electromagnet (Soft Iron Wire)

When an electromagnet fed by AC mains is held above a soft iron wire, the electromagnet attracts the soft iron wire whenever magnetic flux peaks, regardless of whether the current is in the positive or negative half-cycle.

Since AC current reaches maximum amplitude twice in every complete cycle, the soft iron wire is attracted twice per cycle. Thus, the wire vibrates at twice the mains frequency:

$$\nu_{\text{wire}} = 2f$$

Equating to the fundamental frequency of the wire:

$$2f = \frac{1}{2l}\sqrt{\frac{T}{m}} \implies f = \frac{1}{4l}\sqrt{\frac{T}{m}}$$

3. Graphical Method of Determination

Since $T = Mg$, we have $l \propto \sqrt{T}$. A graph of $\sqrt{T}$ on the y-axis against resonant length $l$ on the x-axis gives a straight line passing through the origin.

$$\text{Slope } k = \frac{\sqrt{T}}{l} = 2f\sqrt{m} \implies f = \frac{k}{2\sqrt{m}} = \frac{\text{Slope}}{2\sqrt{m}}$$

Alternatively, a graph of $T$ against $l^2$ yields a straight line with slope $S = \frac{T}{l^2} = 4 m f^2$, giving $f = \frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{S}{m}}$.

4. Linear Mass Density Determination ($m$)

The linear mass density $m$ of the wire is determined by measuring its diameter $d = 2r$ with a micrometer screw gauge and using the density of the wire material $\rho$:

$$m = \frac{\text{mass}}{\text{length}} = \pi r^2 \rho = \frac{\pi d^2 \rho}{4}$$

📝 Viva Voce Interactive Examination

Test your conceptual understanding for practical exams: Score: 0 / 10
Q1. What is the standard frequency of alternating current (AC) mains in India and the UK?
Explanation: In India, the UK, and Europe, standard domestic AC power is generated at 50 Hz (50 complete cycles per second). In North America (USA/Canada), the standard is 60 Hz.
Q2. In Method A (AC current in wire with permanent horseshoe magnet), why does the wire vibrate at the AC frequency $f$?
Explanation: The magnetic Lorentz force $F = I L \times B$ changes direction every time current $I$ alternates sign. Therefore, one full cycle of AC produces one full cycle of mechanical force (frequency = $f$).
Q3. Why does a soft iron wire placed under an AC electromagnet vibrate with a frequency of $2f$?
Explanation: Magnetic force on unmagnetized soft iron is proportional to $B^2$. Regardless of polarity (+ or - current), soft iron is always attracted. Thus, it experiences two force peaks per cycle ($2f$).
Q4. Why is the paper rider placed at the midpoint between the two knife-edge bridges?
Explanation: In fundamental mode standing waves, nodes (zero motion) occur at the fixed bridges and the single antinode (maximum displacement) is at the exact center. The paper rider flutters and is thrown off when placed at this antinode.
Q5. What is the purpose of the hollow wooden soundboard with holes in the sonometer?
Explanation: A thin wire vibrating in air has small surface area and produces very faint sound. The bridges transmit wire vibrations to the large hollow wooden box, which sets a large volume of enclosed air into resonance, greatly magnifying the audible sound.
Q6. What type of graph is obtained when $\sqrt{T}$ is plotted on the y-axis against resonant length $l$ on the x-axis?
Explanation: According to the law of tension, $f = \frac{1}{2l}\sqrt{\frac{T}{m}} \implies \sqrt{T} = (2f\sqrt{m}) \cdot l$. This represents $y = mx$, a straight line through the origin whose slope is $2f\sqrt{m}$.
Q7. Why is a step-down transformer used instead of connecting the sonometer directly to 220V mains?
Explanation: Direct 220V connection is fatal and would pass enormous currents through the low-resistance wire, burning it instantly. A 2V–6V step-down transformer ensures complete student safety while maintaining the exact mains frequency.
Q8. If the suspended mass $M$ is quadrupled ($4 \times$), by what factor does the resonant length $l$ change?
Explanation: Since $l = \frac{\sqrt{T}}{2f\sqrt{m}} \propto \sqrt{M}$, quadrupling $M$ makes $l' \propto \sqrt{4M} = 2\sqrt{M} = 2l$. The resonant length doubles!
Q9. Which instrument is used to determine the diameter and linear mass density of the sonometer wire?
Explanation: A screw gauge has a least count of $0.01\text{ mm}$ (or $0.001\text{ cm}$), which provides the high precision required to accurately measure thin wires ($0.4\text{ to }0.8\text{ mm}$ diameter).
Q10. What is meant by "resonance" in this experiment?
Explanation: Resonance is the physical phenomenon where an oscillating system driven by a periodic external force responds with maximum amplitude when the driving frequency equals the natural fundamental frequency of the system.

📊 Experimental Error & Uncertainty Propagation

1. Formula for Frequency

$$f = \frac{1}{2l} \sqrt{\frac{T}{m}} = \frac{1}{2l} \sqrt{\frac{M g}{\pi r^2 \rho}} = \frac{1}{2l r} \sqrt{\frac{M g}{\pi \rho}}$$

2. Fractional Uncertainty Propagation

Taking logarithms and differentiating, the maximum fractional error in frequency is given by:

$$\frac{\Delta f}{f} = \frac{\Delta l}{l} + \frac{1}{2}\frac{\Delta M}{M} + \frac{\Delta r}{r} = \frac{\Delta l}{l} + \frac{1}{2}\frac{\Delta T}{T} + \frac{1}{2}\frac{\Delta m}{m}$$

3. Typical Instrument Limits of Error:

  • Metre Scale: $\Delta l = 0.1\text{ cm} = 0.001\text{ m}$ (Least count)
  • Screw Gauge: $\Delta d = 0.01\text{ mm} \implies \frac{\Delta r}{r} = \frac{\Delta d}{d} \approx \frac{0.01}{0.60} \approx 1.67\%$
  • Slotted Weights: $\Delta M \approx 0.005\text{ kg} \implies \frac{\Delta M}{M} \approx 0.5\%$
