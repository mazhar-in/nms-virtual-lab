Theory: Volume of an Irregular Lamina

1. Principle: An irregular lamina is a thin sheet whose surface boundaries are not described by standard geometric shapes. Its volume is given by:

$$V = \text{Surface Area } (A) \times \text{Mean Thickness } (t_{\text{mean}})$$

2. Thickness ($t$) Measurement using Screw Gauge:

The screw gauge operates on the principle of a precision micrometer screw. The Least Count ($\text{LC}$) is given by:

$$\text{LC} = \frac{\text{Pitch}}{\text{Total Circular Scale Divisions } (N)}$$

For zero error $e$:

$$\text{True Thickness } t = (\text{PSR} + n \times \text{LC}) - (\pm e)$$

Measurements are taken at 5 distinct points ($P_1$ to $P_5$) across the sheet to account for non-uniform rolling:

$$t_{\text{mean}} = \frac{t_1 + t_2 + t_3 + t_4 + t_5}{5}$$

3. Determination of Area ($A$) using Millimeter Graph Paper:

Place the irregular lamina on a graph sheet and trace its boundary. Count the squares inside:

  • $n_1$ = Number of complete full squares (area $1\text{ cm}^2$ each).
  • $n_2$ = Number of squares with more than half of their area within boundary (counted as $1\text{ cm}^2$).
  • Squares with less than half of their area within boundary are neglected.
$$\text{Surface Area } A = (n_1 + n_2) \times 1\text{ cm}^2$$

4. Resultant Volume:

$$V = A \times \frac{t_{\text{mean}}\text{ (in mm)}}{10} \quad (\text{in cm}^3)$$
