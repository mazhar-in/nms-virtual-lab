Theory: Downward Force along an Inclined Plane

1. Principle: Consider a heavy cylindrical roller of weight $W = M g$ resting on a smooth inclined plane of inclination angle $\theta$ with the horizontal. The weight $W$ acts vertically downwards through its center of gravity.

2. Resolution of Forces: Resolving the weight $W$ into two mutually perpendicular components:

  • Component perpendicular to the inclined plane: $R = W \cos\theta$, balanced by the normal reaction of the plane.
  • Component parallel to the inclined plane pointing downwards: $F_{\text{down}} = W \sin\theta$.
$$F_{\text{down}} = W \sin\theta$$

3. Eliminating Pulley Friction via Reversible Limits: Let $f$ be the small frictional force of the roller and pulley:

  • When weights in the pan are increased such that the roller just starts moving upward: $W_1 = W \sin\theta + f$.
  • When weights in the pan are decreased such that the roller just starts rolling downward: $W_2 = W \sin\theta - f$.
  • The mean balancing force is: $$F = \frac{W_1 + W_2}{2} = W \sin\theta$$

4. $F\text{–}\sin\theta$ Graph Analysis: A graph plotted between the downward force $F$ on the y-axis and $\sin\theta$ on the x-axis is a straight line passing through the origin. Its slope represents the total weight of the roller:

$$\text{Slope} = \frac{\Delta F}{\Delta(\sin\theta)} = W$$

Comparing this slope with the measured weight $W$ on a beam balance verifies the relationship.
