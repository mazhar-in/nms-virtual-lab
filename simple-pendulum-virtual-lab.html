Theory: Simple Pendulum & Second's Pendulum

Principle: A heavy spherical bob suspended by a light, inextensible thread from a rigid support undergoes simple harmonic motion for small angular displacements ($\theta \le 15^\circ$). The restoring torque is proportional to the angular displacement:

$$\tau = - m g L \sin\theta \approx - m g L \theta$$

Time Period ($T$):

$$T = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{L}{g}} \implies T^2 = \frac{4\pi^2}{g} L$$

where $L$ is the effective length measured from the point of suspension to the center of gravity of the bob ($L = l + h + r$).

Second's Pendulum: A pendulum whose period of oscillation is exactly $2\text{ seconds}$ (takes $1\text{ s}$ for each swing from one extreme to the other). Its length $L_s$ is given by:

$$2 = 2\pi \sqrt{\frac{L_s}{g}} \implies L_s = \frac{g}{\pi^2} \approx \frac{980}{9.8696} \approx 99.3\text{ cm}$$

$L\text{–}T^2$ Graph: A plot between effective length $L$ on the x-axis and $T^2$ on the y-axis is a straight line passing through the origin. The acceleration due to gravity is determined from the slope:

$$\text{Slope} = \frac{\Delta (T^2)}{\Delta L} = \frac{4\pi^2}{g} \implies g = \frac{4\pi^2}{\text{Slope}}$$
